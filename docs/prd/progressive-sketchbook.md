# PRD: From a quick Sketchbook overview to a book

**Status:** Draft for review. This proposal does not select a storage model or tool API.

## Problem

A reader who asks “What does this codebase do?” may only want a sentence about its purpose and a short list of features. The same reader may later ask how one feature works, where the behavior is implemented, or how several parts fit together. The first answer should be useful on its own. The follow-up should add detail without making the reader start over or lose earlier content.

Today, the [Viewer displays one document's ordered content blocks](../../src/web/app.tsx), and the [write tool replaces the full document on a matching revision](../../README.md#use). Authors can preserve existing blocks in a replacement, but that requires them to carry those blocks forward. Deck slides already support a visual walkthrough; they are not a substitute for chapters that each need their own prose, code, and diagrams.

## Goals and non-goals

- Let a reader get a quick answer without opening or navigating a book. Let an author expand that answer into a book when the subject warrants it.
- Keep the overview and previously published content available as authors add chapters, sections, or diagrams.
- Keep the Viewer read-only and preserve the existing access and session boundaries. Continue to support authoring from Pi and MCP.
- Do not add nested chapters or sections, a browser editor, public hosting, version history, or restore in the first release. Do not require an automatic conversion based on text length or diagram count.

## Success measures

- **Quick codebase tour:** A reader can identify the codebase's purpose and main features without opening another page. If they ask how one feature works, the follow-up adds the verified behavior and relevant source locations without displacing the overview.
- **Chat software walkthrough:** From one Viewer entry, a reader can open four substantive chapters in order: **How a message travels** (sequence diagram), **System architecture** (component responsibilities), **API design** (send, history, subscribe, and retry), and **Database design** (conversations, members, message order, and deduplication). A reader can link directly to the API retry section. Appending a new chapter preserves the first four.
- **Release evidence:** Record authoring results from Pi and MCP, and browser checks for navigation and full chapter readability at desktop and 390px. Keep save success, render success, readability, and human acceptance separate, as in the [current verification guidance](../acceptance.md).

## Dependencies and open questions

1. **Storage and concurrency:** Decide whether a book groups standalone documents or uses a new document format. Compare preservation of existing IDs and links, atomic changes to the chapter order, and the behavior of concurrent edits. Record this choice in an ADR after evaluating the alternatives.
2. **Authoring API:** Decide how Pi and MCP address the target chapter, section, block, or deck and which revisions guard each operation. Keep the current full-replacement contract for existing clients.
3. **Viewer links:** Define stable identifiers and URL handling for chapters and sections without widening Viewer access or exposing its capability token. Links are not required to survive Viewer shutdown; reopening after shutdown may issue a new link.
